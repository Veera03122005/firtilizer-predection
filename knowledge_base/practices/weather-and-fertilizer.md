# Weather, rain, irrigation and fertilizer

## Before heavy rain

Do not apply urea or other nitrogen fertilizer when heavy rain is forecast within the next day or two. Heavy rain washes fertilizer off the surface (runoff) and carries nitrate below the root zone (leaching), especially on sandy soils. Light rain after application is helpful because it moves urea into the soil.

## If it rains heavily just after application

- Some nitrogen is likely lost. Watch the crop for 1–2 weeks: if lower leaves turn pale yellow, a small extra urea top dressing may be needed. Ask your local agriculture officer before adding more.
- Phosphorus and potassium placed in the soil at sowing are rarely lost by rain.
- Drain waterlogged fields quickly; waterlogging causes nitrogen loss as gas and root damage.

## Hot and dry conditions

- Urea on dry, hot soil loses nitrogen to the air as ammonia. Apply to moist soil and incorporate it or irrigate lightly afterwards.
- Avoid fertilizer application during the hottest part of the day; prefer early morning or evening.
- Do not apply fertilizer to moisture-stressed crops; irrigate first.

## Soil moisture

The best time to apply fertilizer is when the soil is moist but not saturated (it crumbles in the hand). Under rainfed conditions, apply top dressing only after enough rain has moistened the soil.

## Humidity and foliar sprays

Spray foliar nutrients in the morning or evening when humidity is higher and wind is low. Do not spray if rain is expected within a few hours.

## Temperature and humidity in the app

The app's model uses temperature, humidity and soil moisture as inputs, but in its training data these values have very little effect on which fertilizer is chosen; the nutrient values matter most. Use the weather mainly to decide when to apply.
