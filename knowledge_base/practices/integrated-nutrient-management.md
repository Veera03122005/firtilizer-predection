# Organic options and integrated nutrient management

## What integrated nutrient management (INM) means

INM combines chemical fertilizers with organic manures, crop residues, green manures and biofertilizers. It keeps soil healthy, improves the response to fertilizer, and can reduce chemical fertilizer use, usually by about a quarter when good organic inputs are used regularly. It is recommended over chemical-only fertilization.

## Organic manures

- **Farmyard manure (FYM):** well-decomposed cattle dung and litter. Typically 10–12.5 tonnes per hectare, spread and ploughed in 2–3 weeks before sowing. It supplies small amounts of all nutrients (roughly 0.5% N, 0.2% P₂O₅, 0.5% K₂O) and greatly improves soil structure and water holding.
- **Compost:** decomposed crop residues and farm waste; used like FYM.
- **Vermicompost:** produced by earthworms; richer than FYM (about 1–2% N). Commonly 2.5–5 tonnes per hectare.
- **Oil cakes** (neem cake, groundnut cake): concentrated organic nitrogen; neem cake also helps against some soil pests.

Only use well-decomposed manure; fresh dung can burn seedlings and carries weed seeds.

## Green manuring

Grow dhaincha (Sesbania) or sunhemp for 45–50 days, then plough it into the soil before transplanting or sowing the main crop. It adds nitrogen and organic matter and is widely used before paddy.

## Biofertilizers

| Biofertilizer | Crops | Benefit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rhizobium | Pulses, groundnut, soybean | Fixes nitrogen in root nodules |
| Azotobacter | Upland cereals, vegetables, cotton | Fixes nitrogen in the soil |
| Azospirillum | Paddy, millets, maize, sugarcane | Fixes nitrogen near roots |
| Phosphate-solubilising bacteria (PSB) | All crops | Makes soil phosphorus more available |
| Blue-green algae / Azolla | Flooded paddy | Adds nitrogen in the field water |

Use as seed treatment or soil application just before sowing. Keep packets in shade and use before the expiry date. Do not mix directly with chemical fertilizers or fungicides in the same solution.

## Can I go fully organic?

It is possible, but yields may fall in the first seasons while soil fertility builds up, and large quantities of manure are needed. For most farmers, combining organic manure with a reduced, soil-test-based chemical dose gives the best results and cost.
