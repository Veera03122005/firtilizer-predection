# Fertilizer application methods and timing

## Basal application

Fertilizer applied at or just before sowing or transplanting is called the basal dose. Phosphorus and potassium are normally applied fully as basal because phosphorus hardly moves in soil and roots need it early. Mix basal fertilizer into the soil at the last ploughing, or place it in furrows.

## Top dressing

Fertilizer applied to the standing crop is top dressing. Nitrogen (usually urea) is top-dressed in one to three splits at the growth stages when the crop needs it most: tillering, knee-high, panicle initiation, squaring or the first irrigation, depending on the crop. See each crop guide for exact stages.

## Why split nitrogen

Nitrogen is easily lost by leaching, runoff and as gas. Giving it in splits matches supply to crop demand, reduces losses, avoids early lush growth that attracts pests, and usually increases yield from the same amount of fertilizer.

## Placement methods

- **Band or furrow placement:** fertilizer placed 4–5 cm to the side of and below the seed. Best for phosphorus and for row crops. Keep fertilizer out of direct contact with seed.
- **Broadcasting:** spreading evenly over the field. Common for urea top dressing; follow with incorporation or irrigation.
- **Side dressing:** placing fertilizer in a band beside crop rows, then covering with soil (maize, cotton, sugarcane).
- **Foliar spray:** dissolving fertilizer in water and spraying leaves; used for quick correction of micronutrient deficiency or 2% DAP / urea sprays at flowering. It supplements, never replaces, soil application.

## Good timing practice

- Apply to moist soil, not to dry or waterlogged soil.
- Apply in the morning or evening for top dressing; avoid the hottest hours.
- Check the weather forecast: do not apply just before heavy rain.
- Irrigate lightly after urea application where possible.
- Weed the field before top dressing so the crop, not weeds, gets the fertilizer.
