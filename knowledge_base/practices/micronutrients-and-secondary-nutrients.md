# Secondary nutrients and micronutrients

## Sulphur (S)

Needed for oil and protein formation; important for oilseeds and pulses. Deficiency: young leaves uniformly pale yellow. Sources: single super phosphate, 20-20-0-13, gypsum, ammonium sulphate, sulphate of potash. A typical dose is 20–40 kg sulphur per hectare for oilseeds.

## Calcium (Ca)

Needed for cell walls and, in groundnut, for pod filling. Gypsum at 400–500 kg/ha at flowering is the standard calcium source for groundnut. Acidic soils may need lime based on a soil test.

## Magnesium (Mg)

Part of the green pigment chlorophyll. Deficiency: yellowing between the veins of older leaves, sometimes reddening (seen in cotton). Magnesium sulphate as a soil application or 1% foliar spray corrects it.

## Zinc (Zn)

The most common micronutrient deficiency in Indian soils, especially in rice, maize and wheat on alkaline or intensively cropped soils. Symptoms: pale bands or spots on young leaves, stunting ("khaira" in rice, "white bud" in maize). Zinc sulphate at about 25 kg/ha as a basal dose, typically once in two or three seasons, or a 0.5% zinc sulphate foliar spray for quick correction.

## Boron (B)

Important for flowering and seed set in oilseeds, pulses, cotton and vegetables. Deficiency causes poor flowering, hollow stems and poor fruit/seed set. Apply only on a soil-test or local recommendation basis, because the gap between enough and toxic is small.

## Iron (Fe)

Deficiency shows as yellowing between the veins of young leaves, common in calcareous soils and upland rice. Ferrous sulphate foliar sprays are used for correction on local advice.

## General advice

Micronutrients are needed in small quantities; more is not better and some are toxic in excess. Apply them only when a soil test or clear symptoms confirm a deficiency, following local recommendations.
