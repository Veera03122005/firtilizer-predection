# NPK basics and nutrient deficiency symptoms

## What N, P and K mean

- **N — Nitrogen:** leaf and stem growth, green colour, tillering, protein.
- **P — Phosphorus:** roots, early vigour, flowering, seed and grain formation, early maturity.
- **K — Potassium:** strong stems, water use, disease resistance, grain filling and quality.

These three are the major (primary) nutrients. Sulphur, calcium and magnesium are secondary nutrients; zinc, boron, iron and others are micronutrients needed in small amounts.

## How to read a fertilizer grade

A grade like 10-26-26 lists, in order, the percentage of nitrogen (N), phosphorus as P₂O₅ and potassium as K₂O. So a 50 kg bag of 10-26-26 contains 5 kg N, 13 kg P₂O₅ and 13 kg K₂O. A fourth number, as in 20-20-0-13, is usually sulphur.

Recommendations are given in kg of nutrient per hectare (N, P₂O₅, K₂O), not kg of fertilizer. To convert: kg of fertilizer = kg of nutrient ÷ (percentage ÷ 100). Example: 60 kg P₂O₅ from DAP (46%) = 60 ÷ 0.46 ≈ 130 kg DAP.

## Nitrogen deficiency

- Older (lower) leaves turn pale yellow first, starting from the tip; the whole plant looks light green.
- Stunted growth, thin stems, few tillers or branches, early maturity with low yield.

## Phosphorus deficiency

- Stunted plants with poor roots; dark green leaves, sometimes purplish on older leaves and stems, especially in cool weather.
- Delayed flowering and maturity, poor grain and seed formation.

## Potassium deficiency

- Yellowing then browning and drying along the edges and tips of older leaves ("scorch").
- Weak stems and lodging, small or shrivelled grains, more disease.

## Sulphur deficiency

- Young (upper) leaves turn uniformly pale yellow, unlike nitrogen deficiency which starts on old leaves.
- Common in oilseeds and pulses on light soils.

## Zinc deficiency

- Young leaves show pale bands or interveinal yellowing; short internodes, small leaves.
- Rice: rusty-brown spots ("khaira"); maize: white bands ("white bud").

## Important

Symptoms can look similar for different causes (disease, waterlogging, pests). A soil test and a visit from the local agriculture officer give a reliable diagnosis.
