# Fertilizer safety, storage and overuse

## Storage

- Store bags in a dry, covered, well-ventilated place, on a raised platform or wooden pallets, away from walls and floor moisture.
- Keep fertilizer away from seeds, food, animal feed, pesticides and fuel.
- Keep out of reach of children and livestock. Urea and other fertilizers are harmful if eaten by animals.
- Keep bags closed; urea absorbs moisture and forms lumps. Break lumps before use instead of throwing them away.
- Do not store fertilizer in living rooms or kitchens.

## Handling

- Wear gloves and avoid touching your face while handling fertilizer; wash hands and face afterwards.
- Avoid inhaling dust when opening bags or broadcasting on windy days.
- Do not mix fertilizers with pesticides unless the label or local advice says it is safe.
- Clean sprayers and equipment after use.

## Signs of overuse

- Scorched or burnt leaf tips and margins, wilting after application ("fertilizer burn").
- Very dark green, lush, soft growth; lodging; increased pests such as planthoppers and aphids.
- Delayed flowering and maturity, poor grain quality.

## Environmental effects of overuse

- Nitrate leaching contaminates wells and groundwater.
- Runoff into ponds and streams causes algae growth.
- Long-term overuse of nitrogen fertilizers can acidify soil, and an imbalanced N-P-K supply degrades soil health.

Follow soil-test-based doses, split nitrogen, and combine with organic manure to avoid these problems.

## If someone swallows fertilizer

Seek medical help immediately and show the bag label to the doctor. Do not induce vomiting unless a doctor advises it.
