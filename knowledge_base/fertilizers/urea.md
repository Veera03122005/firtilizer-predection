# Urea (46-0-0)

## What it is

Urea is the most widely used nitrogen fertilizer in India. It contains 46% nitrogen (N) and no phosphorus or potassium, so its grade is written 46-0-0. In India it is sold as neem-coated urea in 45 kg bags. The neem coating slows the conversion of urea in soil, which reduces nitrogen loss and discourages misuse for non-farm purposes.

## When it is recommended

Urea is recommended when the crop mainly needs nitrogen: the soil's phosphorus and potassium are adequate but nitrogen is low. Nitrogen drives leaf and stem growth, green colour, tillering in cereals, and grain protein.

Typical signs that nitrogen is short: older (lower) leaves turn pale yellow first, plants are stunted with thin stems, and cereals produce few tillers.

In the app's training data, Urea is the answer whenever the Nitrogen value is high (35–42 on the dataset scale) and Phosphorous and Potassium are both 0. See "About this system" for what these dataset values mean. Phosphorus and potassium, if still required by the crop, are supplied by DAP and MOP in the dosage table.

## How to apply

- Apply urea in split doses, not all at once. A common pattern is part at sowing (basal) and the rest as one or two top dressings at the crop's key growth stages. The crop guides give the exact split.
- Apply to moist soil, then incorporate it into the soil or follow with light irrigation. Urea left on the dry soil surface, especially on alkaline or sandy soils in hot weather, loses nitrogen to the air as ammonia.
- In transplanted paddy, drain the field to a thin film of water before broadcasting urea, and do not apply into deep standing water. Re-flood after one to two days.
- Broadcast evenly between rows for top dressing; keep granules off wet leaves to avoid leaf scorch.
- Do not apply on waterlogged soil or just before heavy rain: nitrogen is lost by leaching and runoff.

## How much

The amount depends on the crop's nitrogen requirement and how much nitrogen other fertilizers (such as DAP or complex fertilizers) already supply. One kilogram of urea supplies 0.46 kg of nitrogen, so 100 kg of nitrogen needs about 217 kg of urea. The app's dosage table calculates urea after subtracting the nitrogen already provided by other products.

## Cautions

- Too much urea causes lush, soft growth that attracts sucking pests (for example brown planthopper in rice), lodging (plants falling over), and delayed maturity.
- Excess nitrogen leaches as nitrate into groundwater.
- Do not mix urea with seed or place it in direct contact with germinating seed.
- Urea is poisonous to livestock if eaten; store bags where animals cannot reach them.

## Alternatives

- Ammonium sulphate (21% N plus 24% sulphur) for sulphur-deficient soils and oilseeds.
- Organic sources: farmyard manure, compost, vermicompost, green manure, and biofertilizers such as Azotobacter or Azospirillum can reduce, but usually not fully replace, urea needs. See integrated nutrient management.
