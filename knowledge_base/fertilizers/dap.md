# DAP — Diammonium Phosphate (18-46-0)

## What it is

DAP contains 18% nitrogen (N) and 46% phosphorus as P₂O₅, with no potassium. It is the most common phosphorus fertilizer in India and is sold in 50 kg bags. Because it carries both N and P, the nitrogen in DAP counts toward the crop's total nitrogen requirement.

## When it is recommended

DAP is recommended when the soil is low in phosphorus relative to the crop's needs. Phosphorus is essential for root development, early vigour, flowering, seed and grain formation, and early maturity.

Signs of phosphorus shortage: poor root growth, stunted plants, dark green or purplish colour on older leaves (especially in cool weather), delayed flowering and maturity, and poor grain filling.

In the app's training data, DAP is the answer when Phosphorous is high (35–42 on the dataset scale), Nitrogen is moderate (10–15) and Potassium is 0. See "About this system" for what these dataset values mean. If the crop also needs potassium, the dosage table adds MOP; remaining nitrogen comes from urea.

## How to apply

- Apply DAP as a basal dose, at sowing or transplanting. Phosphorus moves very little in soil, so it must be placed where roots will grow; top dressing DAP on the surface later is much less effective.
- Best method: place in bands or furrows 4–5 cm below and to the side of the seed, or broadcast and mix into the soil at the last ploughing.
- Do not put DAP in direct contact with seed. Around the dissolving granule the soil becomes temporarily alkaline and releases ammonia, which can damage germinating seeds and seedlings.
- In black and calcareous soils, phosphorus gets fixed by calcium; band placement and adding organic manure improve availability.

## How much

One kilogram of DAP supplies 0.46 kg of P₂O₅ and 0.18 kg of N. For example, 60 kg/ha of P₂O₅ needs about 130 kg/ha of DAP, which also supplies about 23 kg/ha of nitrogen. The app computes DAP from the crop's P₂O₅ requirement and adjusts urea for the nitrogen that DAP supplies.

## Foliar use

For pulses and cotton, a 2% DAP foliar spray (2 kg DAP soaked overnight in 100 litres of water, using the clear solution) at flowering is a common recommendation to support pod or boll setting. Follow local advice for the number of sprays.

## Cautions

- DAP does not supply potassium or sulphur; on soils low in these, add MOP and a sulphur source.
- Continuous use of only DAP and urea, without potassium and organic matter, leads to nutrient imbalance and falling yields over time.

## Alternatives

- Single super phosphate (SSP, 16% P₂O₅ with sulphur and calcium) — good for oilseeds and pulses that need sulphur.
- NPK complexes such as 14-35-14, 28-28-0 or 20-20-0-13 supply phosphorus together with other nutrients.
- Phosphate-solubilising bacteria (PSB) and rock phosphate with organic manure on acidic soils.
