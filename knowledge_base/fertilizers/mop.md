# MOP — Muriate of Potash (0-0-60)

## What it is

Muriate of potash (MOP) is potassium chloride. It contains 60% potassium as K₂O and about 47% chloride, with no nitrogen or phosphorus. It is sold in 50 kg bags and is the main potassium fertilizer in India. The app uses MOP to supply any potassium the crop still needs after the recommended fertilizer.

## Why potassium matters

Potassium strengthens stems (less lodging), improves water use and drought tolerance, helps resistance to diseases and pests, and improves grain filling, fruit size, sugar content and quality.

Signs of potassium shortage: yellowing and then browning ("scorching") along the edges of older leaves, weak stems, lodging, and shrivelled grains.

## How to apply

- Usually applied as a basal dose with phosphorus at sowing, mixed into the soil.
- On light sandy soils, potassium can leach; split it into basal and one top dressing.
- For long-duration crops like sugarcane and banana, split applications are common.

## How much

One kilogram of MOP supplies 0.60 kg of K₂O. For example, 40 kg/ha of K₂O needs about 67 kg/ha of MOP.

## Cautions

- Because of its chloride content, MOP is not preferred for chloride-sensitive crops such as tobacco (poor leaf burning quality) and some fruits and vegetables. Use sulphate of potash (SOP) for these crops.
- Black (vertisol) soils are often naturally rich in potassium; follow a soil test before applying large potash doses.

## Alternatives

- Sulphate of potash (SOP, 50% K₂O with sulphur) for chloride-sensitive crops.
- NPK complexes with potassium: 10-26-26, 14-35-14, 17-17-17.
- Wood ash and crop residues return some potassium to the soil.
