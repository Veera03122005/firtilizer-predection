# Wheat — fertilizer guide

## Nutrient requirement

General recommendation for irrigated, timely-sown wheat: **120 kg N, 60 kg P₂O₅ and 40 kg K₂O per hectare** (about 48.6 : 24.3 : 16.2 kg per acre). Rainfed or late-sown wheat needs less; follow your state's recommendation and soil test.

## Application schedule

| Stage | Nitrogen | Phosphorus | Potassium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basal, at sowing | 50% | 100% | 100% |
| First irrigation / crown root initiation (CRI), 20–25 days after sowing | 50% | — | — |

The crown root initiation stage is the most critical time for both water and nitrogen in wheat. Apply the urea top dressing just before or with the first irrigation so that it moves into the root zone.

## Worked example from the app

For 2 acres of wheat with Medium soil-test ratings and DAP as the recommended fertilizer, the app calculates about 105.6 kg DAP, 54.0 kg MOP and 169.8 kg urea in total. At sowing: all the DAP and MOP plus about 64 kg urea. At the first irrigation: about 106 kg urea.

## Zinc and sulphur

- Zinc deficiency (yellowing between veins of young leaves, stunted growth) is common in rice–wheat systems; apply zinc sulphate at about 25 kg/ha at sowing on deficient soils.
- Sulphur deficiency causes uniform yellowing of young leaves; gypsum or ammonium sulphate corrects it.

## Common mistakes

- Placing DAP on the surface after sowing: phosphorus must go into the soil at sowing.
- Delaying the first irrigation and nitrogen beyond about 25 days: tillering and yield drop.
- Applying urea on dry soil without irrigation: high ammonia loss.
