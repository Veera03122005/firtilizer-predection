# Paddy (Rice) — fertilizer guide

## Nutrient requirement

General recommendation for irrigated transplanted paddy (medium-duration varieties): **120 kg N, 60 kg P₂O₅ and 40 kg K₂O per hectare**. Per acre this is about 48.6 kg N, 24.3 kg P₂O₅ and 16.2 kg K₂O. Short-duration varieties and rainfed rice need less; hybrids and long-duration varieties may need more. Follow your state's recommendation and soil test.

In the app, the model label is "Paddy" (shown as "Paddy (Rice)").

## Application schedule

| Stage | Nitrogen | Phosphorus | Potassium |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basal, at transplanting (last puddling) | 50% | 100% | 100% |
| Active tillering, 20–25 days after transplanting | 25% | — | — |
| Panicle initiation, 45–50 days after transplanting | 25% | — | — |

On light soils, potassium can also be split: half basal, half at panicle initiation.

## Applying in a flooded field

- Incorporate basal fertilizers into the puddled soil before transplanting.
- For urea top dressing, drain the field to a thin film of water, broadcast urea evenly, and re-flood after one to two days. Do not broadcast urea into deep standing water, and avoid letting water flow out of the field just after application.

## Zinc and other nutrients

- Zinc deficiency is common in rice soils, especially alkaline and continuously flooded soils. Symptoms ("khaira"): rusty-brown spots on older leaves, stunted plants and uneven growth 2–4 weeks after transplanting. Apply zinc sulphate at 25 kg/ha at the last puddling on zinc-deficient soils (often once in two or three seasons).
- Green manuring with dhaincha or sunhemp before transplanting adds nitrogen and organic matter.
- Blue-green algae and Azospirillum are useful biofertilizers for rice.

## Common mistakes

- Applying all nitrogen at transplanting: much is lost and plants grow lush early, inviting pests.
- Excess nitrogen: soft growth, lodging, and higher brown planthopper and blast problems.
- Ignoring potassium: weak stems and poor grain filling.
