# Soil types and fertilizer use

The app uses five soil types: Sandy, Loamy, Black, Red and Clayey. Soil type changes how fertilizer behaves, especially how quickly nutrients are lost and how well they are held.

## Sandy soil

- Large particles, low organic matter, drains quickly, holds little water or nutrients.
- Nitrogen and potassium leach easily below the root zone. Give nitrogen in more, smaller splits, and split potassium into basal plus one top dressing.
- Organic manure (FYM, compost) improves water and nutrient holding.
- Irrigate lightly and often; heavy irrigation just after fertilizer washes nutrients down.

## Loamy soil

- A balanced mix of sand, silt and clay; good drainage and good nutrient holding. Usually the best agricultural soil.
- Standard recommendations and normal split schedules work well.
- Maintain organic matter with regular manure or crop residues.

## Black soil (vertisol, "black cotton soil")

- High clay content; swells when wet and cracks deeply when dry. Holds a lot of water.
- Usually rich in potassium, calcium and magnesium, but often low in nitrogen, phosphorus, organic matter and zinc.
- Phosphorus gets fixed by calcium in these alkaline soils: place phosphorus in bands near the seed and add organic manure.
- A soil test may show that potash can be reduced. Avoid fertilizer application when the soil is waterlogged.

## Red soil

- Formed from iron-rich rocks; red colour from iron oxides. Often light-textured, low in organic matter, nitrogen and phosphorus, and slightly acidic to neutral.
- Holds less water; fertilizer responds well when moisture is adequate.
- Phosphorus can be fixed by iron and aluminium in acidic red soils; band placement and organic manure help. Very acidic red soils may need liming on a soil-test basis.
- Crusting after rain can affect seedling emergence; organic matter reduces it.

## Clayey soil

- Fine particles; holds water and nutrients well but drains slowly and can become waterlogged.
- In waterlogged conditions nitrogen is lost as gas (denitrification). Ensure drainage; apply nitrogen when the soil is moist but not saturated.
- Hard to work when too wet or too dry; incorporate basal fertilizer at the right moisture.
- Good for paddy because it holds standing water.
