# Frequently asked questions from farmers

## Can I apply all the fertilizer at once to save labour?

Phosphorus and potassium can usually all go at sowing. Nitrogen (urea) should be split as shown in the schedule; applying it all at once wastes a large part of it and can harm the crop.

## Can I mix urea, DAP and MOP before applying?

They can be mixed just before basal application and applied the same day. Do not store mixtures for long, because urea absorbs moisture and the mix becomes sticky. Do not mix fertilizers with seed.

## I missed the top-dressing date. Should I still apply urea?

Apply it as soon as possible if the crop is still growing actively and has not reached the next stage (for example flowering in cereals). Very late nitrogen gives little benefit and can delay maturity. Ask your local agriculture officer if unsure.

## Why does the app recommend a complex fertilizer instead of urea and DAP?

The recommended fertilizer comes from the machine-learning model, based on the nutrient pattern you entered. A complex supplies several nutrients in one granule. The dosage table then fills any remaining gap with DAP, MOP or urea so the crop's full requirement is met.

## Can I use a different fertilizer from what the app recommends?

Yes, as long as the crop gets the same amounts of N, P₂O₅ and K₂O. For example, SSP can replace DAP (about three times the quantity, plus extra urea for the missing nitrogen). The crop's nutrient requirement matters more than the brand or product.

## How much fertilizer do I need for one acre?

One acre is about 0.4 hectare, so per-acre quantities are about 40% of per-hectare quantities. Enter your area and unit in the app and the dosage table shows totals and bags for your farm.

## Will more fertilizer give more yield?

Only up to the recommended level. Beyond it, yield rises little or even falls, pests increase, and money is wasted. Follow the recommendation adjusted to your soil test.

## Is organic manure enough?

Manure improves soil health and reduces fertilizer needs, but on its own it usually cannot supply a high-yielding crop's full nutrient demand. Combine manure with a reduced, soil-test-based chemical dose.

## What should I do with leftover fertilizer?

Store it in a dry place in a closed bag, off the floor, away from children, animals, seeds and food. Use it in the next season; check that it has not become hard or wet.

## The leaves are yellow even after I applied urea. Why?

Possible reasons: nitrogen lost to heavy rain or waterlogging, deficiency of sulphur or zinc (which look similar), root damage, disease or pests. Check which leaves are yellow (old leaves suggest nitrogen; young leaves suggest sulphur or zinc) and consult your local agriculture officer.

## Where can I get my soil tested?

At the nearest government soil testing laboratory, Krishi Vigyan Kendra (KVK), or through the Soil Health Card programme. Collect the sample after harvest, before fertilizing.
